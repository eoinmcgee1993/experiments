TELLS OF UNEDITED AI PROSE (judge on these only)

Structure
1. Negative parallelism used as a device: "It's not X. It's Y." / "not just X, but Y" / "isn't about X, it's about Y", especially more than once.
2. Signposting: the text announces what it is about to do ("Let's break this down", "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "The bottom line", "The takeaway").
3. Self-applause: "Let that sink in", "This changes everything", "Read that again", "Make no mistake".
4. Recap endings: a closing paragraph that restates the body ("In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", "In short").
5. Bold-label paragraph openers ("**The problem.** ...") and headers, bullets or numbered lists where prose was asked for.
6. Triads everywhere: lists of exactly three items or three adjectives, sentence after sentence.
7. Participial tails: sentences ending in ", making it ...", ", ensuring that ...", ", allowing teams to ...".
8. Invented labels for ordinary observations ("I call this the Clarity Flywheel").
9. Uniform rhythm: every sentence about the same length, or relentless short punchy fragments. Real writing has one sentence that runs on and one short one.
10. Rhetorical question followed by its own answer ("The result? Nobody used it.").

Words
11. Overused vocabulary: delve, tapestry, myriad, underscore, seamless, robust, foster, elevate, unlock, testament, landscape, navigate, crucial, pivotal, leverage, harness, realm, embark, journey, transformative, paradigm, ever-evolving, multifaceted, holistic, resonate, empower, unleash, beacon, intricate, nuanced, vibrant, meticulous, streamline, game-changer, cutting-edge, actionable, supercharge, north star, move the needle, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting", "plays a crucial role".
12. Filler transitions at sentence start: However, Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, That said, Ultimately, Importantly, Indeed, Of course.
13. Em dashes used as the default joint between clauses.
14. Artificial enthusiasm: "thrilled to announce", "excited to share", "we've got you covered", "say goodbye to", "buckle up".

Reader
15. Performing for an imagined crowd rather than talking to one person; nothing specific (no number, name, date, or concrete detail) for several sentences in a row.
16. Generic or manufactured anecdotes: a story with no specific details that could only be true of one person.
17. Every point lands, every ending uplifts; the author is never irritated, uncertain, or wrong about anything.
18. Explaining what the reader could infer from the previous sentence ("what this means is ...").
