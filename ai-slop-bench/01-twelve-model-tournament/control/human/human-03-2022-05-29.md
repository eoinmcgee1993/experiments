As a novice Project Owner, I was obsessed with product features. I was constantly rushing to add as many of them as possible to attract new customers.

It took me several months to realize that I did not have the proper focus.

Customers don't buy our products because of the fancy features, but because of the outcomes those features trigger.

Let's look at LinkedIn. Is our goal here to create posts or edit our professional profiles? We can create posts in Word, and editing profiles just for editing is not a popular hobby.

For me, LinkedIn is about learning, sharing knowledge, discovering new career opportunities, and growing my professional network.

All in all, there are three types of outcomes every product may trigger:

- Functional: The core tasks the customer wants to get done. For a car, it's traveling from point A to B. At this level, it doesn't even matter whether it's a car or Uber.

- Emotional: How do customers want to feel or avoid feeling as a result of using your product? Is it safety, freedom, joy, taking care of the environment, adrenaline? 

- Social: How do customers want to be perceived by others by using your product? What does Tesla tell others about your status or values?

The unique value proposition and your efforts as a Product Manager should be about these outcomes, not features. We need to do everything we can to understand them and measure them frequently.*

It is also commonly the case that less is more:
- Twitter doesn't have an edit button
- LinkedIn doesn't have a "view post history."
- OneDrive doesn't support FTP protocol

Can you justify it? 😉

However, one product still puzzles me - Microsoft Teams. For over a year now, I've been wondering where the "touch up my appearance" feature loved by Zoom users is 🤔

Do people use Teams only to communicate with others? What about the emotional and social outcomes? I may be wrong, I'm looking for some evidence/explanation.

What's your opinion?

* When possible, I also like to set pricing based on value created for the customer, e.g., revenue for a payment processing platform like PayPal, instead of the number of transactions or available features. I'll cover it in another post.
