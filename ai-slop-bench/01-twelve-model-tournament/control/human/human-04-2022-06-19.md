Most content on product management is focused on what "to do." 

However, the biggest challenge for me was unlearning the behaviors I learned at the beginning of my career. 

Here are the top 3:

- Stop obsessing over the solutions. Instead, focus on understanding the problem, your customers, and your users. What are their concerns, goals, aspirations, fears, and ambitions? What are their values and beliefs? What are the outcomes they deeply care about?

- Resist the temptation to define tasks. Regardless of your experience, this is not the PM's job. Empower your teams, co-create the vision and goals (why, what), and let them decide how best to achieve them.

- Embrace the uncertainty. No matter how much time you spend planning, the solution will never be perfect. Create a safe environment in which teams run experiments. Use prototypes to get maximum validated learning with minimal effort. Deliver often, in small iterations, to get stakeholder and customer feedback.

I started as a software developer. Of course, every person is different, but the pattern remains the same. The skills that allow us to advance as experts in a specific field often do not translate into product management skills.
