Unfortunately, almost nobody is doing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, which, by definition, is the only possible Scrum. 

Let it sink in.

Scrum has died.

I don't believe it's only because "bad organizations" do not want to empower people.

Scrum's dogmatism and unwillingness to receive feedback from the community essentially contributed to this. Ideas on how to improve Scrum bounce off the wall. Decisions are made in secret.

Where are the transparency and openness? What about co-creation? This is not what Agile leadership is about.

In my opinion, Scrum lacks continuous product discovery, which makes it the solution space framework. "Inspired," "Continuous Discovery Habits," or "Sprint" are classics every PM should read.

Another aspect is releasing value. I'd argue that usable Increment is not enough. In order to provide value, the Increment must be released to the customers. 

Don't get me wrong. Most Scrum ideas are great. I'm a great advocate of what's in the framework.

At the same time, ignoring the problem space and the importance of releasing value (and the associated feedback loops!) makes me wonder:

"Is it intelligently incomplete?"

If so, why for so many teams does it become a feature factory framework?

And even more importantly:

"Han can we improve it?"
