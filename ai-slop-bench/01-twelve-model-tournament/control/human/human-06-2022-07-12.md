In product-led growth, UX is king. And yet, many #product managers struggle to measure and improve it. Here comes Google's HEART framework 👇

It's measures five elements:

✅ 1. Happiness. It is defined as the user's attitude or satisfaction. This is a highly subjective metric. We can collect the data, e.g., with surveys.

💡 Be aware that changes may negatively impact Happiness but do not necessarily have long-term implications.

✅ 2. Engagement. Measures how users interact with the application. For a streaming platform, it may be a total time spent watching videos. Other metrics may include regularity of use or usage patterns.

💡 Make sure you measure activity connected with the value generated for the customers and the business. E.g., we do not want to increase the time required to configure an account.

✅ 3. Adoption (customer acquisition). The number of users you gain over a period of time.

💡 One of the most critical metrics for product-led companies is the time to value (TTV). It's time before customers experience the value promised after purchasing the product (e.g., free trial). Building the shortest path to value may boost your Adoption.

✅ 4. Retention. Your product should be sticky enough to attract and retain customers for a very long time.

💡 The best way to do it is to keep your customer happy. Changes in Happiness often precede changes in Retention.

✅ 5. Task Success. It encompasses several traditional behavioral metrics of user experience, such as efficiency (e.g., time to complete a task), effectiveness (e.g., percent of tasks completed), and error rate.

💡 You can do it remotely on a large scale by assigning users specific tasks. It's good practice to compare those results with your competition (benchmarking).

---

You can use it in three steps:

✅ 1. Goals. First, identify your product goals. What are you trying to achieve with this product or feature?

✅ 2. Signals. How can you determine if you have succeeded or failed in each goal? Sometimes failure is easier to identify than success (e.g., the task was not completed).

✅ 3. Metrics. Finally, consider how you can map signals to specific metrics (HEART).

HEART metrics have helped Google's product teams make better, user-centric, data-driven decisions. Now you too can benefit from it :)
