Too much #Lean can undermine your product efforts.
 
Stop applying it without reflection.
 
Lean puts much emphasis on reducing waste and costs. If product managers focus on it too much, things go terribly wrong.
 
Let's look at two areas of waste in lean manufacturing:
 
1. Unused talent ("underutilization of people")
 
Knowledge work is different from work on the production line. 100% utilization is never a good solution. The inevitable multitasking and constant context switching can drastically lower people's productivity.
 
We don't get paid for being fully utilized (feature factory). We get paid for delivering valuable outcomes. To do that, we need time to pause and think.
 
You should be "utilized" at about 50 to 60 percent to provide maximum value for your organization. More than that is a waste of talent.
 
2. Extra-processing
 
Lean defines extra-processing as quality higher than required by the customer. It makes sense for screws or chairs. The product fulfills its function, which may be the only thing customers expect.
 
In #productmanagement, we need a different approach. Think about iPhone. Do people buy it only to make calls or take photos? Emotional and social outcomes can be more important than the core functionalities of the product.
 
Customers don't just want their needs to be met. They want to be delighted.
 
-
 
Don't get me wrong. Lean and #Agile can coexist. But it's important to remember that Lean comes from manufacturing. We should use it carefully.
 
#Scrum doesn't implement Lean in its entirety. In my opinion, you shouldn't either.
