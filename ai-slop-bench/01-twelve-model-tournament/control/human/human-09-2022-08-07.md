The #Agile Manifesto was a success.

But it is outdated.

It did its job; it helped us move away from the waterfall. I support many of its values and principles.

At the same time, I think it wasn't well prepared. And most importantly, it has not been updated since 2001.

What holds on, and what's missing?

Let's start with the "Working software is the primary measure of progress" principle. Working software is just an output. How do we know it delivers any value? It's a perfect recipe for a feature factory. Today we understand we need to focus on the outcomes.

The Agile Manifesto also doesn't mention product discovery. Building a feature to find out nobody needs it, that it doesn't scale due to technical limitations, or that it doesn't produce value for the business is a waste, no matter how much you "Welcome that change." We can mitigate those risks by validating our ideas with the help of prototypes.

The Agile Manifesto also lacks many necessary ingredients for a successful product organization:

- Data and information are not present. Those are strategic assets every PM should understand and leverage.

- Leadership is not even mentioned. In this context, "Build projects around motivated individuals" is just wishful thinking. 

- DevOps couldn't be included, as the term was born in 2008 - 7 years later.


The Agile Manifesto may be outdated, but the Agile principles and values existed long before it was created. And they have evolved significantly since 2001.

I do not doubt that agility will thrive. Nothing, not even fake Agile frameworks like #SAFe, can change it.
