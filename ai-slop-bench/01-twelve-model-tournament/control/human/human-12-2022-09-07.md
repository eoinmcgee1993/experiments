Dropbox grew from 100K to 4m users in 15 months.

With no sales reps! How is that even possible?

Dropbox used the viral engine of growth. Like a virus, the viral engine places the growth emphasis on increasing person-to-person transmission. This type of growth is, by design, exponential.

There are three common strategies you may consider:

1. Inherent - When the product itself requires sharing (e.g., DropBox, Miro)

2. Artificial - Sharing is connected with the reward system (e.g., Revolut, many banks)

3. Word-of-mouth - When customers recommend the product, they tried to others (e.g., Uber, Canva)

One key metric PMs need to track is the viral coefficient, which is the number of new customers that an existing customer generates. If the viral coefficient > 1, your business can grow like crazy.

TLTR 

Let's say every customer sends 20 friend invitations with a conversion rate of 15% (3 colleagues accept invitations). Then your viral coefficient is 3.0.

In the example above, after only 13 cycles, you'll have 3¹² = 531,441 customers. But if the viral coefficient is 2.5, the result would be only 2.5¹²=59,605 customers.

As we can see, even tiny changes in the viral coefficiency will cause dramatic changes in the results. Companies using the viral engine must focus on the viral coefficient more than anything else.

- 

The viral engine of growth helped Dropbox to grow impressively. However, in my opinion, nothing can be done without a product that creates real value. 

Talk to your customers. Try to emphasize them. What are their problems, needs, and desires? What do they care about? What are they afraid of? What do they not know about themselves? 

Finally, what is the cheapest way to test your assumptions? (Dropbox used the Video MVP)
