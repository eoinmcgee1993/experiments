Product management is, at its heart, risk management.

PM should be obsessed with those questions:

- Value. Will our customers desire it?
- Usability. Will they be able to use it?
- Viability. Can our business support it?
- Feasibility. Is it possible?
- Ethic. Should we do it?

To precise it further, desirability is not enough. Your solution must solve problems significantly better than others to persuade customers to switch. So I like to replace the first question with:

- Value. Will our customers love it?

Be aware that Agile frameworks, like Scrum, largely ignore PM. PM is a black box that generates ideas and places them in the product backlog. Therefore, mastering Agile and Scrum is not enough to succeed.

Here is what can help:

- Understanding customers, market, and business in depth. You need to become an expert in each of those areas.

- Determining your target customers, their underserved needs, and the value proposition. Testing your product idea with the help of the MVP experiment.

- Continuously discovering what to build: analyzing qualitative and quantitative data (e.g., customer interviews, Pendo); collaborating with designers and engineers to ideate possible solutions; identifying the riskiest assumptions and testing them with the help of prototypes.

- Measuring key product metrics, understanding trends, and optimizing your product using analytics.

Hope that helps.
