Last quarter I shipped a feature I was genuinely proud of. People kept saying our reporting felt limited, so I built a flexible query builder that let them mix metrics, add custom filters, and save their own views. I spent nights on the UI and made sure the queries actually ran fast. We launched it with a short note in the product and a line in the changelog.

Two weeks later I looked at the numbers. Almost nobody had opened it. A few clicks on day one, then nothing. I sat there feeling stupid. All that work and it might as well have been invisible.

I messaged a few customers I thought would love it. Their answers were consistent and a little embarrassing. They did not want more flexibility. They wanted the three reports they already used to load faster and to export them without extra steps. The fancy builder solved a problem they did not have.

What stuck with me is this. I had been talking to the product more than I had been talking to people. I assumed a request for better reporting meant they wanted power. It meant they wanted less friction on the work they already did every week. Shipping something is easy to celebrate. Watching it sit unused is the part that actually teaches you.

Next time I will talk to five users before I write a single ticket. I will ask what they did last Tuesday instead of what they wish they could do someday. And I will treat usage, not launch day, as the real finish line.
