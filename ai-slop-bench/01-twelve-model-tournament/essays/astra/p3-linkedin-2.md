We shipped the feature customers asked for.

Then almost nobody used it.

It was a customizable reporting dashboard. In discovery calls, people wanted more filters, more charts, and more ways to slice their data.

So we built them.

The launch went smoothly. The demo looked great. The team was proud.

A month later, the dashboard was mostly empty.

My first instinct was to blame discoverability. Maybe we needed better onboarding, another announcement, a more prominent button.

Instead, we sat down with customers and asked them to show us how they prepared their weekly updates.

That’s when we saw what we’d missed.

They didn’t want to explore their data. They wanted to answer three predictable questions before a Monday meeting—and paste the answers into an email.

We had built flexibility. They needed a shortcut.

The lesson wasn’t “don’t listen to customers.”

It was: **a feature request is the start of discovery, not the end of it.**

Now, before committing to a solution, I want to understand:

- What happened the last time someone faced this problem?
- How did they solve it without us?
- What would make changing that routine worthwhile?
- What behavior would tell us we’d actually helped?

And wherever possible, we test the smallest version inside the customer’s existing workflow.

Shipping that dashboard taught me more than a smooth launch ever could.

We hadn’t failed to build what people requested.

We’d failed to understand what they were trying to get done.
