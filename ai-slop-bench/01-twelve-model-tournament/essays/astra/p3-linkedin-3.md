The feature shipped on time. The launch post got plenty of 🎉 reactions.

Almost nobody used it.

We’d spent weeks building a customizable dashboard because customers kept asking for “better visibility.” We interpreted that as more filters, more charts, and more control.

What they actually needed was much simpler:

“Tell me what changed since yesterday and whether I need to do anything.”

We built a place to explore. They wanted a signal to act.

The uncomfortable part? We had done customer research. We had tickets, quotes, and a beautifully organized requirements document.

But we’d asked people what they wanted to see—not what they were trying to get done.

After launch, one customer shared their screen with us. They opened the dashboard, scanned it, then exported the data into the same spreadsheet they’d always used.

That five-minute interaction taught us more than weeks of internal debate.

We stopped adding options and tested a short daily summary instead: what changed, what needed attention, and a link to take action.

It was less impressive in a demo. It fit much better into someone’s day.

The lesson wasn’t “don’t build dashboards.”

It was this: **A customer request is the beginning of discovery, not the end of it.**

Now, before committing to a feature, I want to understand:
- What triggers the need?
- What does the person do today?
- What would they stop doing if our solution worked?

Shipping something nobody used was frustrating. But it changed how I define progress.

A shipped feature is an output. A changed behavior is evidence that it helped.
