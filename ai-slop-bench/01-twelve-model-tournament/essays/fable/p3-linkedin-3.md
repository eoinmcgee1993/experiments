**We spent eleven weeks building a feature that got used 47 times. Total. Across 12,000 accounts.**

Here's what happened, and what I actually learned.

Last year my team shipped an "advanced export builder." Custom fields, saved templates, scheduled delivery, the works. We were proud of it. The demo got applause in the all-hands. Sales said customers had been asking for it for years.

Ninety days later I pulled the usage numbers expecting to write a victory lap. Instead I found that almost nobody had touched it, and the handful who did mostly opened it once and never came back.

My first instinct was to blame discoverability. We'd buried it two menus deep, surely that was it. So we added a banner. Then an onboarding tooltip. Then an email campaign. Usage barely moved.

So I did the thing I should have done in week one: I called ten customers who had explicitly requested "better exports" and asked them to show me their workflow.

Every single one had the same problem, and it wasn't the one we solved. They didn't want a *powerful* export. They wanted to stop exporting at all. They were pulling data out of our product to paste it into a slide for their boss every Monday. The export was a symptom. The disease was that our reporting page didn't answer the one question their boss kept asking.

We had taken "customers want better exports" at face value and built a Ferrari for people who wanted to stop driving.

**The lesson I keep coming back to:** A feature request is a customer's *guess* at a solution. It is not the problem. When someone says "I need X," the most valuable question is not "how do we build X?" but "what will you do the moment X exists?" If they can't answer that vividly, you haven't found the real need yet.

A few things I do differently now:

- **Every roadmap item needs a "day after" story.** Not "users can schedule exports," but "Priya opens her laptop Monday morning and the number is already in front of her." If we can't write that sentence, we're not ready to build.
- **Talk to requesters *before* scoping, not after shipping.** Ten 20-minute calls would have cost us two days. Skipping them cost us eleven weeks.
- **Watch, don't just ask.** People describe what they think they do. Screen-shares show what they actually do. The gap is where the real product lives.
- **Define "success" before the kickoff.** We never agreed on what adoption would look like. So we couldn't recognize failure until it was obvious to everyone.

The export builder is still in the product. It works fine. Nobody uses it, and that's okay. It taught me more than most of the features that "succeeded."

If you're a PM, engineer, or designer with a similar story, I'd genuinely love to hear it. The features that flopped quietly are the ones nobody writes case studies about, and I think that's a shame.
