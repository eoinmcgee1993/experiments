I spent 6 weeks shipping a feature our users begged for.

Custom dashboards. Drag-and-drop widgets. Export to PDF. Beautiful, fast, on time.

Launch day: 🎉

Week 1: 3% adoption.
Week 4: basically zero.

I was crushed. Then I got curious and called 15 of the users who'd requested it.

Here's what I learned:

**1. They didn't want dashboards. They wanted answers.**
What they really meant by "custom dashboards" was "tell me if we're on track on Monday morning without digging." We built a power tool. They needed a 30-second email digest.

**2. "Everyone wants it" meant 3 loud voices.**
We'd confused feedback volume with feedback breadth. We never validated with data, prototypes, or the silence from the other 97%.

**3. We shipped output, not outcome.**
Success was "launch on time," not "solve the problem weekly." We celebrated merging PRs instead of changing behavior.

Now I live by this: Fall in love with the problem, not your solution.

Before I build anything, I ask:
→ What's the job to be done in the user's words?
→ How will we know it's working 30 days after launch?
→ What's the smallest version that could deliver value?

That failed feature? We replaced it with a simple Monday Slack summary. 68% weekly open rate. Took 4 days to build.

Sometimes the best product work is unbuilding your assumptions.
