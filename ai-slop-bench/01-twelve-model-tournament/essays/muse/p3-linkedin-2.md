I shipped a feature nobody used. It still stings.

Two years ago, my team kept hearing the same request: "We need better reporting."

So we built it. A beautiful dashboard. Filters, exports, scheduled emails, the works. We polished it for 6 weeks and launched it with a big announcement.

Crickets.

After a month, 3% had tried it once. 0% stuck around. When we finally sat down with users, we heard the painful truth:

When they said "more reporting," they meant "just give me the answer." Not another chart to interpret.

We had solved our interpretation of the request, not their actual job-to-be-done.

That flop changed how I build forever:

**1. Fall in love with the problem, not the solution.**
We spent 6 weeks building and 0 days shadowing users. Now I won't let my team design anything until we've watched 5 users struggle with the problem live.

**2. Ship the embarrassing version first.**
We could have validated value in 2 days with a manual weekly email with insights. Instead we built automation for a workflow nobody wanted.

**3. Define failure upfront.**
We never agreed on: "If X% don't retain in Y weeks, we kill it." So the zombie feature lived in our codebase for a year, adding maintenance cost and guilt.

The most expensive code is the code nobody uses. The best PMs I know aren't the best builders — they're the fastest learners.
