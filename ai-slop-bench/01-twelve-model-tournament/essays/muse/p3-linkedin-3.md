We spent 3 months building a feature nobody used.

It’s still the best lesson of my career. 👇

Two years ago, my team was convinced our users wanted advanced custom dashboards.

Sales kept asking for it. Competitors had it. It sounded impressive on a roadmap.

So we built it. Filters, widgets, drag-and-drop, exports — the whole thing. We polished every pixel.

Launch day came. We announced it with pride.

...crickets.

After 30 days, exactly 47 people had tried it. After 60 days, only 3 were still using it. We’d built a Ferrari for people who just wanted a faster bike.

It hurt. But it taught me something I’ll never forget:

**Fall in love with the problem, not your solution.**

We hadn’t solved a real, painful, frequent problem. We’d built what *we* thought was cool, without watching a single user struggle first.

Now I do 3 things differently before I ship anything:

**1. Talk first, build second.** 5 customer conversations > 50 internal opinions.
**2. Ship the smallest test.** Could we have validated this with a fake door test or a simple CSV export?
**3. Define success + kill criteria upfront.** If X% don’t use it in Y days, we kill it. No ego.

That flop made me a better builder. I now get more joy from deleting unused code than shipping new code.

Have you ever shipped something nobody used?

What did it teach you?
