We spent weeks shipping a feature we were proud of.

The launch was clean. The metrics were brutal: nobody used it.

At first, I blamed discovery. Then I blamed timing. Eventually, I admitted the truth: we fell in love with an idea, not a customer problem.

The feature solved something that felt important in a meeting, but didn’t create enough friction in everyday use. We asked users if they wanted it, and they said yes — because being nice to a product team is free. We treated interest as demand and prototypes as proof.

That taught me the hardest lesson in product work: shipping is not validation. Behavior is validation.

Now I try to run a small pre-flight before building anything:

- Who has this problem repeatedly?
- What are they doing today?
- What would make them pay, switch, or change their workflow?
- What’s the smallest version that creates real risk of rejection?

If we can’t answer those quickly, we don’t build the feature. We investigate.

The best product decision I’ve made wasn’t a feature that changed the world. It was the discipline to stop confusing effort with evidence.

A feature nobody used didn’t waste time if it taught the team to listen better.
